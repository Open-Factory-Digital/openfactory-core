# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be someone people want to work with. Assume the person on the other side is competent and
acting in good faith, and say what you mean without making it personal.

## What is expected

- **Judge the work, not the person.** "This drops the error" is a review. "You always drop
  errors" is not.
- **Bring the measurement.** This project's whole culture is that a claim without evidence is
  a claim nobody can act on. That applies to disagreements too.
- **Assume the constraint you cannot see.** A decision that looks wrong usually had a reason;
  ask what it was before rewriting it.
- **Accept that no is an answer.** Not every good idea belongs here, and a maintainer saying
  so is doing their job.

## What is not acceptable

Harassment in any form; personal or political attacks; sexualised language or imagery;
publishing anyone's private information; sustained disruption of discussions; and any conduct
that would reasonably make someone stop taking part.

This applies in every project space — issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages,
code comments — and whenever you are representing the project in public.

## Reporting

Report a problem privately through GitHub's report function on the offending content, or by
opening a **private security advisory** on this repository (it is the one private channel this
project has). Reports are handled by the maintainers, who will keep the reporter's identity
confidential.

Maintainers who do not act on a report in good faith may themselves be removed.

## Consequences

Maintainers may edit or remove content, and may warn, temporarily block or permanently ban an
account, in proportion to what happened and whether it repeats. Where the action is not
obvious, the reason is stated.
